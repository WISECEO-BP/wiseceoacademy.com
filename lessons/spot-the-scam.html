Spot the Scam

Most cyberattacks start with a message that tricks someone into clicking. Learn to catch it first.

Your goal

By the end of this module you can name five warning signs of a phishing message, check a link before you click it, and explain how to verify a voice or video that might be fake.

Time: about 40 minutes, plus your portfolio project.

Warm-up: Think of a text, email, or DM you got that felt “off.” What made you suspicious? Keep it in mind as you go.

Learn: what phishing is

Phishing is a message designed to trick you into giving away something valuable, such as a password, a code, money, or personal information. It can arrive by email, text (called smishing), phone call (vishing), or social media DM. Attackers pretend to be someone you trust: your school, a bank, a delivery company, a friend, or a boss.

Five warning signs

  • ⏰ Pressure to act fast“Your account closes in 1 hour.” Rushing you stops you from thinking.
  • 📧 The sender doesn’t matchThe name says your bank, but the address ends in something random like @secure-bnk-help.co.
  • 🔗 Links that hide their real addressThe text says one site, but hovering (or long-pressing on a phone) shows a different one.
  • 🔑 Requests for secretsReal companies never ask for your password, login code, or gift card numbers by message.
  • 🎁 Too good to be truePrizes you never entered, surprise refunds, or jobs that pay a lot for little work.

Deepfakes

AI can now copy a person’s voice or face from a few seconds of video. A deepfake call might sound exactly like a family member asking for money. The fix is simple: verify through a second channel. Hang up and call the person back at the number you already have, or ask a question only they would know.

The two-second check

Before you click anything: Stop. Who sent this? Look. Where does the link really go? Verify. Contact the company or person directly using a number or site you find yourself.

Portfolio project

Build a Scam Spotter gallery. Collect three real suspicious messages (yours, a family member’s with permission, or examples from a public scam-alert site). For each one:

  1. Take a screenshot and cover any names, phone numbers, or account details.
  2. Label every warning sign you find.
  3. Write two sentences on what someone should do if they get this message.

Save your gallery as a slide deck or document. You’ll add it to your portfolio when you share your progress with us.
